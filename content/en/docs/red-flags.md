A quiet house does not mean everything is OK. Look for these signs that something might be wrong.

- They are on the phone constantly.
- They panic or get scared when they cannot check it.
- Their habits change.
- They retreat.
- They show signs of depression or excessive self-blame.
- They show signs of abuse or neglect.

None of these prove anything on their own. A teenager who withdraws might be dealing with school, or friends, or nothing at all. What matters is a change from how your kid normally is, particularly one that lines up with a new app, a new contact, or a new device.

## What to do

Start with the followers list. Go through it together and ask who each person is. Watch how their demeanor shifts from name to name, because the reaction usually tells you more than the answer does.

Keep your own reaction flat. A kid who expects to lose their phone or get yelled at stops telling you things, and that costs you far more than the app did.

### If explicit photos are involved

Explicit images can often be taken down, though not in every case.

- [Take It Down](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org/) is a free service from the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children. It helps remove nude or sexually explicit images of someone who was under 18 when the image was made. The image itself never leaves the device, because the service works from a digital fingerprint of it.
- Report child sexual exploitation to the [NCMEC CyberTipline](https://report.cybertip.org/), or call 1-800-843-5678.

Tell your kid plainly that they are not in trouble and that this can be fixed. Shame is what keeps kids quiet, and staying quiet is what lets it continue.
