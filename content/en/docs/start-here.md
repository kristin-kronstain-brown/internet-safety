Teach your kids how to live safely in a constantly connected world.

Be direct. Kids notice when an adult softens a topic, and they read that as a signal not to raise it again.

## A simple family framework

You can teach almost everything about internet safety by encouraging your kids to think through these five questions:

- Who is asking?
- Why do they want this?
- How does this make me feel?
- What could happen if I share this?
- Would it be okay if this became public?

That framework scales from kindergarten through adulthood. The rest of this guide uses those questions as a foundation.

## Decide on house rules together

Rules hold up better when kids help write them. Treat this as a starting list.

- Treat internet safety as a recurring topic, not a one-time talk. Families can run refreshers the same way workplaces run security training.
- No phones in rooms at night.
- No devices behind locked doors. Predators want kids to hide.
- Accept friend requests only from people you know, not friends of friends.
- Always be respectful to others online.
- Tell a parent when you see something that makes you uncomfortable.

## Review your kid's accounts together

Agree up front that you will look, and how often. A review your kid expects feels very different from one that ambushes them.

- Do you know your kid's followers? Ask who each one is.
- Read the comments people leave for your kid.
- Know what is being downloaded.
- Get into your kid's business without scaring them into hiding things.

Watch for renamed and disguised apps. Both the icon and the name can be changed, so a calculator on the home screen is not always a calculator.
