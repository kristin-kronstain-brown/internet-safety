Someone who wants something from a kid tends to work a conversation the same way every time. Knowing the pattern is what lets a kid notice it while it is happening.

This page covers two halves of one pattern: the three tactics someone uses, and the stages they move through to get there.

## The three tactics

1. **Fraud.** Lying to get something, such as pretending to be another kid or promising a prize that does not exist.
2. **Coercion.** Pressure and threats, such as "if you don't send another one, I'll post the first."
3. **Force.** Taking the choice away, by seizing an account or a device.

## How someone takes control of a conversation

Control is rarely taken all at once. It gets built in stages, and each stage looks reasonable on its own.

1. Zero in on what the kid wants.
2. Build trust, through shared history or rapport.
3. Fill a need. Get control by "providing" financially or with hope.
4. Pull the kid away, isolating them from other people in their life.
5. Abuse, using the three tactics above.
6. Take total control.

Three things make stage 5 easier, and all of them are worth naming out loud:

- AI can generate fake explicit images, so a kid who never sent a photo can still be threatened with one.
- Boys are often won over with digital currency, such as game credits and skins.
- Gifts create obligation. "Your parent won't let you do what you want on your phone? I'll send you another one."

Teach kids not to accept phones, money, or game currency from people they do not know.

## Teach kids basic marketing techniques

A kid who can see through a commercial can see through a predator, because both are running the same play.

- Figure out what people want from you.
- Watch commercials together and talk about what they appeal to, and what they want out of you.

Marketing relies on four basic rules, often called the 4 Ps. A predator uses all four:

1. **Product.** Predators sell themselves as a friend, someone who feels supportive rather than strict.
2. **Price.** Predators are willing to give up something to gain something.
3. **Place.** Predators want a tool that gives them a private conversation.
4. **Promotion.** Predators push, usually with urgency, so the kid acts before thinking.

## Give kids a way out

A kid needs to know what to do, not only what to notice.

- Say no, firmly. Feeling rude is fine. Safety matters more than politeness.
- Block them.
- Remove the app.
- Tell a [trusted adult]({{< relref "/guide/ages-4-6/index.md#lesson-3-trusted-adults" >}}), even if the conversation has been going on for a long time.
