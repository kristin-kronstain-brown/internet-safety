Treat teaching your kids about internet safety like learning to cross a street or answer the door. Most people are fine, but kids need skills to notice problems, protect themselves, and ask for help.

This is not "stranger danger." Strangers can be helpful, too, and a kid who has been taught to fear everyone unfamiliar has learned the wrong lesson.

What you are actually building:

- Confidence
- Critical thinking
- Emotional awareness
- Healthy boundaries
- Knowing when to pause and ask an adult

Kids learn best when:

- Conversations happen regularly
- Adults stay calm and nonjudgmental
- Examples are realistic
- Mistakes are treated as learning opportunities

Each age band below builds on the one before it. Start where your kid is, then revisit the earlier bands as they get older, because the same idea lands differently at 7 than it did at 5.

Every lesson ends with a game you can print and play together. Each one has a **Print this game** button that prints just that game, on one sheet, without the rest of the site. Most need nothing but scissors and a pen. The games for ages 13 and up are card decks and sorting games rather than worksheets, because a teenager will not fill in a worksheet.
